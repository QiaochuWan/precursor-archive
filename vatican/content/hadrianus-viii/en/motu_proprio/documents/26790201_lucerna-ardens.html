APOSTOLIC LETTER

ISSUED MOTU PROPRIO

BY THE SUPREME PONTIFF
HADRIAN VIII

“LUCERNA ARDENS”

ON THE JURIDICAL CONDITION OF THOSE WHO ARE MISSING IN EVENTS OF ANNIHILATION, AND ON CERTAIN NORMS FOR THE ARCHDIOCESE OF SAINT BRIGID

✠

“He was a burning and a shining light.”[1] With these words the Lord praised John, because in the darkness John bore witness to the light that was to come.

From ancient times the Church has used the lamp and the flame as signs of watchfulness. At Kildare, Saint Brigid and her sisters kept a fire that was never allowed to go out; for centuries someone tended it by day and by night, so that passers-by might know that here someone was awake. Today the world that bears her name has been lost to us. What the Church must do is to keep the lamp, and not let it go out.

Last year the Archdiocese of Saint Brigid, together with all the inhabitants of that world, was cut off at once from every spaceway and every channel of communication of the Federation. The observations of the Federal Government and of the Pontifical Academy indicate that this loss belongs to the same class as the anomalous annihilation events that have previously occurred elsewhere. It differs from a misalignment of the ring windows: in the case of a window misalignment, we know that the people are still there and that for the time being they cannot be reached; in this case, we do not know what remains there, nor whether the people there are still alive.

Our Predecessor John XXIV taught in the Encyclical Letter “Nomina eorum” that a person may be struck from the civil register, but cannot be struck from the memory of God.[2] Our Predecessor Leo XVIII, after the loss of Paradise Lost, established by the Encyclical Letter “Exspectantes” the “dioceses of silence”, decreeing that a diocese from which we are cut off is neither suppressed nor merged, but continues to exist in the prayer of the Church.[3]

Annihilation, however, gives rise to questions that earlier law did not foresee. On the one hand, the Church cannot hastily declare that those people are dead, for we have obtained no certain knowledge, and past experience teaches us that what was thought beyond remedy has sometimes been changed in ways we did not expect. On the other hand, neither can the Church leave the spouses, children and relatives who remain outside to wait indefinitely in a state that cannot be known with certainty, and perhaps never will be, without offering them any way forward. Law that attends only to prudence and forgets mercy becomes a yoke; law that attends only to mercy and forgets truth is no longer law.

Canon 1707 of the Codex Golgothanus provides that the presumed death of a spouse may be declared only after the Bishop, having made inquiry, has attained moral certainty of the death, and that mere absence, even for a long time, does not suffice to constitute such certainty.[4] This provision is wise and remains in force. In the case of annihilation, however, the testimonies, signs and reports by which moral certainty is ordinarily attained are almost entirely unobtainable. It is therefore necessary to establish special norms for such cases.

Accordingly, having sought the opinion of the Supreme Tribunal of the Apostolic Signatura, the Tribunal of the Roman Rota, the Dicastery for Bishops, the Pontifical Office of Names and the Pontifical Academy, and having considered the petitions of the faithful of Saint Brigid living abroad, We establish by this Motu Proprio the following.

Art. 1 · Definitions

§ 1. “Annihilation loss”, for the purposes of this Motu Proprio, means the loss of all contact with a world, a habitation or a vessel as a result of an anomalous annihilation event recognised as such by the competent authority of the Federation or by the Pontifical Academy.

§ 2. Annihilation loss is juridically distinct from loss of contact caused by the closing of a spaceway, by a misalignment of windows, or by any other cause whose end can be foreseen. The latter continue to be governed by the Encyclical Letter “Exspectantes” and the related provisions.

§ 3. Whether a given event constitutes an annihilation loss shall be determined by an opinion of the Pontifical Academy, confirmed and published by the Secretariat of State.

Art. 2 · Registers

§ 1. All persons whose whereabouts are unknown as a result of an annihilation loss shall be entered in the registers at every level as “missing (annihilation)”. Until a judgement has been given in accordance with Art. 3 of this Motu Proprio, they may not be entered as “deceased”.

§ 2. No one may, for any reason whatsoever, strike them from the registers of the Church.

§ 3. The Pontifical Office of Names shall request the competent authorities of the Federation to treat the civil registers according to the same principle; should the Federation proceed otherwise, the entries in the registers of the Church remain unaffected.

Art. 3 · Presumed death

§ 1. A petition for the presumption of death of a person lost through annihilation may be submitted only after seven years have elapsed from the loss of contact.

§ 2. Having received the petition, the diocesan Bishop shall conduct an inquiry in accordance with can. 1707 and must hear the written opinion of the Pontifical Academy concerning the event.

§ 3. The Bishop may not declare the presumed death on his own authority, but shall forward the complete acts of the case to the Tribunal of the Roman Rota for review. The declaration may be made only after the Roman Rota has confirmed that the Bishop has attained moral certainty.

§ 4. The petitioner has the right to be represented by an advocate in the review proceedings, and the right to be informed of the substance of the opinion of the Pontifical Academy.

§ 5. A declaration of presumed death has effect in canon law only; its civil effects are governed by the laws of the Federation.

Art. 4 · The return of the missing

§ 1. Should a person whose death has been presumed later return, the subsequent marriage contracted by the spouse under Art. 3 is invalid.

§ 2. Nevertheless, if that subsequent marriage was celebrated in good faith by at least one of the parties, it is to be considered putative; children born of it enjoy the status of legitimate children in accordance with the Code.

§ 3. In dealing with such cases, pastors shall accompany each of the persons concerned with the greatest mercy, having regard to their consciences, their children and their livelihood. Should the diocesan Bishop judge it necessary, he may refer the case to the Holy See.

Art. 5 · Holy orders and offices

§ 1. The see of a diocese in a region subject to annihilation loss is to be considered “impeded” in accordance with can. 412, and not “vacant”.

§ 2. Clerics of that region remain incardinated in their own diocese, and their offices do not become vacant by reason of the loss of contact.

§ 3. Should contact be restored, the Church supplies, in accordance with can. 144, for sacraments celebrated and acts of governance performed there during the loss of contact, wherever doubt arises as to the existence of the requisite power.

Art. 6 · Temporal goods

§ 1. Ecclesiastical goods of a region subject to annihilation loss which are situated outside that region shall be administered by an Apostolic Administrator in absentia appointed by the Holy See.

§ 2. Such goods are to be preserved against the restoration of contact and may not be merged with the goods of any other diocese; they may not be alienated except for the payment of debts or for the care of the faithful of that region living abroad, and then only with the permission of the Holy See.

§ 3. The Apostolic Administrator in absentia shall submit accounts each year to the Secretariat for the Economy, and a summary of those accounts shall be published.

Art. 7 · The faithful living abroad

The pastoral care of the faithful who were outside the region when the annihilation loss occurred shall be provided for by a separate Apostolic Constitution. Until then, they are entrusted to the care of the diocesan Bishop of the place where they reside, and retain their original registration and their liturgical traditions.

Art. 8 · Preaching

No one may preach in the name of the Church that an annihilation event is a punishment from God upon its victims. Any priest or deacon who so preaches shall be admonished by the diocesan Bishop; should he persist after repeated admonition, he may be punished in accordance with the law.

Art. 9 · Scope

§ 1. This Motu Proprio applies to the Archdiocese of Saint Brigid, with effect from the day on which contact with it was lost.

§ 2. This Motu Proprio applies also to events of the same kind that the Pontifical Academy may hereafter recognise.

Art. 10 · Related liturgical provisions

The Dicastery for Divine Worship and the Discipline of the Sacraments shall provide by a separate decree that in the cathedral church of each window diocese a lamp be lit and kept burning until the day when contact with Saint Brigid is restored. This lamp is not lit for the dead, but for those who wait.

We order that everything We have established by this Apostolic Letter issued Motu Proprio have full and stable force, notwithstanding anything to the contrary, even if worthy of special mention, and We establish that it be published in the Acta Apostolicae Sedis and enter into force on the day of its promulgation.

Given at Golgotha, at the Apostolic Palace, on 1 February, the Feast of Saint Brigid, in the year 2679, the second of Our Pontificate.

HADRIAN VIII
