BIOGRAPHY OF THE HOLY FATHER HADRIAN VIII

Pope Hadrian VIII, born Matteo Ferraro, was born on 14 March 2615 in Saint Brendan's Haven, on the high plateau of Golgotha. His father, John Ferraro, was an engineer in the hydroponic farms; his mother, Ruth Ferraro (née Brennan), was a primary school teacher. The eldest of three children, he was baptized in the Church of Saint Brendan and received his primary and secondary education at the parish school.

In 2633 he was awarded a scholarship for students from the colonies, established by the Pontifical Mission Societies, and went to Scorpius Centre to study mathematics at Newman College. He obtained his bachelor's degree in 2637 and, in 2642, a doctorate with a dissertation in analytic number theory, after which he remained at the College as a research fellow.

In 2643 he returned to Earth and entered the Pontifical Seminary of Saint Peter in Avignon, studying philosophy and theology at the Pontifical Theological Faculty of Avignon. On 29 June 2649, the Solemnity of Saints Peter and Paul, he was ordained a priest in the Great Hall of the Palace of the Popes in Avignon and was incardinated in the Archdiocese of Avignon.

After his ordination he served as parochial vicar of the Parish of Saint Agricol in Avignon while continuing his studies, and in 2653 he obtained a doctorate in theology with a thesis entitled The Infinite and the Created: On the Place of the Mathematical Concept of Infinity in the Theology of Creation. For the following thirteen years he taught fundamental theology and the philosophy of nature at the Pontifical Theological Faculty of Avignon, becoming a full professor in 2659 and serving as Dean of the Faculty from 2662 to 2666. During this period he was elected a corresponding member of the Pontifical Academy and was repeatedly invited to take part in the work of the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith on the relationship between faith and science.

On 3 May 2666 Pope Leo XIX appointed him Metropolitan Archbishop of Birmingham. He received episcopal ordination and took possession of the Archdiocese on 22 July of the same year in the Metropolitan Cathedral of Saint Chad in Birmingham, and on 29 June 2667, the Solemnity of Saints Peter and Paul, he received the pallium from Pope Leo XIX. His episcopal motto is taken from Psalm 147: “He counts the stars” (Numerat stellas).

During his ministry in the Archdiocese of Birmingham he devoted particular attention to parish life in the regions of post-war reconstruction, to the pastoral care of migrant families and to the formation of young people and students. He continued to serve as a visiting lecturer in mathematics at the Federal University in New London, teaching introductory courses in number theory and topology to undergraduates. He showed special concern for students from the colonial worlds studying on Earth, and each year at Christmas he welcomed to his residence those who were unable to return home.

On 22 February 2672, the Feast of the Chair of Saint Peter, Pope Leo XIX created him a Cardinal in the Consistory, assigning him the Title of San Clemente in Rome. He was a member of the Dicastery for Bishops, the Dicastery for Culture and Education and the Pontifical Academy.

Pope Leo XIX died on 10 February 2677. On 14 March of that year, at the Conclave convened in Golgotha, Cardinal Ferraro was elected Bishop of Rome and took the name Hadrian, in memory of his Predecessor Hadrian VI, who had likewise come to the Chair of Peter from a university professorship and had set his heart on the reform of the Church. On 19 March, the Solemnity of Saint Joseph, he celebrated Holy Mass before the Co-Cathedral of the Holy Cross in Golgotha for the beginning of his Petrine Ministry.

COAT OF ARMS

Blazon. Per pale gules and azure; in the first, a Latin cross upon three mounts Or; in the second, a harp Or; over all in chief, a lemniscate between two mullets argent. Above the shield, a silver mitre adorned with three gold bands joined by a gold vertical post, with fluttering red infulae fringed with gold and adorned with gold crosses; in front of the infulae, the crossed Petrine keys, gold and silver, linked by a red cord. Motto: NUMERAT STELLAS.

Explanation. In the red field, the three mounts surmounted by a cross are the traditional emblem of Calvary, recalling the Holy Father's homeland of New Golgotha and the heart of the faith: the Cross and the Resurrection. The red recalls the Precious Blood of Christ. The blue field recalls the heights of heaven and is the colour of the Blessed Virgin Mary; the harp is the emblem of Ireland, in memory of the Irish ancestry of the Pope's mother and of the settlers who carried an ancient faith out among the stars. The harp is also the instrument of King David, and signifies the Psalms and the praise of the Church.

The silver lemniscate, which alone spans both fields, is drawn from the Holy Father's theological study of the infinite and the created: the infinity reached by mathematics is but a sign pointing towards the One who is infinite. The two stars echo the motto, taken from Psalm 147: “He telleth the number of the stars: and calleth them all by their names.”[1] These are the same words with which Pope Thomas opened the Encyclical Letter “Inter sidera”.[2]

Upon his election the Holy Father retained the arms he had borne as a bishop, adorned now, according to custom, with the mitre and the keys of Peter.

[1] Ps 147:4.

[2] Cf. Thomas, Encyclical Letter “Inter sidera” (18 May 2290), 1.
